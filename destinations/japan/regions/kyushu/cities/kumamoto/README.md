# Kumamoto

Kumamoto is known for Kumamoto Castle, still under restoration after the 2016 earthquakes and briefly closed again after a magnitude-7-intensity quake on 28 July 2026. Other sights include Suizenji Jojuen garden and the Kumamon mascot. Kumamoto is also the gateway to Mount Aso.

## How to get there

- From Hakata (Fukuoka): Kyushu Shinkansen Mizuho/Sakura, about 32–38 min; ¥5,310 unreserved, ¥5,840 reserved (normal season; ±¥200 off-peak/peak). Since the line fully reopened on 18 Sep 2026 after the July 2026 earthquake, some sections run slower and trips can be up to about 8 min longer.
- From Shin-Osaka: through Sanyo/Kyushu Shinkansen; EX Hayatoku 21 (booked 21 days ahead) Shin-Osaka–Kumamoto ¥15,400 (fare extended in 2026).

## Getting around

- Kumamoto City Tram: flat fare ¥200 per adult since 1 June 2025 (was ¥180). 1-day pass: mobile ¥500, paper ¥700 (children half). The paper 1-day pass and the Wakuwaku 1day Pass stopped being sold on board on 31 March 2025, so buy them elsewhere.

## Seasons

- A strong earthquake (maximum seismic intensity 7) struck Kumamoto Prefecture on 28 July 2026. Kumamoto Castle closed until 13 Aug 2026, the Kyushu Shinkansen fully reopened on 18 Sep 2026, and the Kumamoto–Kagoshima-Chuo section stopped again on 13 Oct 2026 (overhead wire damage, not linked to the quake) and resumed on 14 Oct.
- Suizenji Jojuen is a cherry-blossom spot. The Hosokawa Gyobutei garden is known for autumn leaves, late Nov–early Dec.
- Mount Aso: JMA eruption alert level 2 (restricted around the crater, about 1 km from Nakadake first crater) remained in force in late Oct 2026, so crater viewing is closed.
