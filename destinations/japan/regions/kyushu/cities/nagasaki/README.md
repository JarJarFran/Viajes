# Nagasaki

Nagasaki is a port city on a narrow inlet in western Kyushu and capital of Nagasaki Prefecture. During Japan's period of isolation it was one of the very few ports open to Dutch and Chinese traders, which shaped its churches, Chinatown and cuisine, and on 9 August 1945 it became the second city destroyed by an atomic bomb.

## How to get there

- From Fukuoka (Hakata): Relay Kamome limited express (about 60 min) to Takeo-Onsen, then Nishi-Kyushu Shinkansen (about 25 min) to Nagasaki; covered by the Japan Rail Pass.
- From Tokyo by train: Tokaido/Sanyo Shinkansen to Hakata, then Relay Kamome + Nishi-Kyushu Shinkansen; about 7 h with Nozomi, around ¥27,000 one way.
- From Tokyo by air: Haneda–Nagasaki by JAL, ANA and Solaseed, Narita–Nagasaki by Jetstar; about 2 h flight. Regular JAL/ANA fare about ¥59,000, discount ¥15,000–29,000; Solaseed about 20% cheaper; Jetstar from about ¥8,000. Lowest fares seen for Nov–Dec 2026: Jetstar ¥8,300, Solaseed ¥13,910, JAL ¥14,370, ANA ¥15,980.
- Nagasaki Airport is about 30 km from the centre: airport bus to Nagasaki Station 45 min, ¥1,200, every 20 min.

## Getting around

- Trams (Nagasaki Electric Tramway, 4 lines) reach most sights, every 5–8 min from 6:00 to 23:00: flat ¥150 per ride (board at the rear, pay when exiting at the front); 1-day pass ¥600.
- Transfers between lines at Shinchi-Chinatown or Shiminkaikan are free only when paying with an IC card (Suica, Icoca, Nimoca, etc.) and boarding within 30 min; paper transfer tickets were abolished in April 2021.
- The centre lies about 1 km (5 min by tram) southeast of Nagasaki Station; the Peace Park is about 10 min north by tram lines 1 or 3.
- Nagasaki Bus city buses are mainly useful for Mount Inasa: Nagasaki Station to the ropeway's lower station 5 min, ¥160; to Inasayama bus stop 15 min, ¥190.

## Local food

- Chanpon — noodles with fried seafood, pork and vegetables in a rich pork/chicken bone broth, Chinese-rooted and best tried in Shinchi Chinatown.
- Sara udon — meat and vegetables in a thick sauce over crispy fried noodles (Chinatown restaurants, typically 11:00-15:00 and 17:00-21:00).
- Kakuni manju — slow-simmered soy-braised pork belly in a soft steamed bun, sold as street food and in restaurants.
- Toruko rice — pilaf, spaghetti and a breaded pork cutlet with curry sauce on one plate.
- Castella — sponge cake introduced by Portuguese missionaries, sold in long boxes as a souvenir.

## Seasons

- Nagasaki Lantern Festival around Chinese New Year (2027: 5–21 February), centred on Shinchi Chinatown.
- Nagasaki Kunchi festival of Suwa Shrine every 7–9 October.
- Mount Inasa ropeway usually stops for maintenance from around early June to early July.
- Gunkanjima landings are cancelled or boats do not sail in bad weather or high waves.
