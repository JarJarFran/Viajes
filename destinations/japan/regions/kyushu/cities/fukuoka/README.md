# Fukuoka

Fukuoka is Kyushu's largest city and a historic harbour city close to the Asian mainland, formed in 1889 by merging the port town of Hakata with the castle town of Fukuoka. It is known for its yatai food stalls, Hakata ramen and the Hakata Gion Yamakasa festival, and Hakata Station is the main rail hub for Kyushu.

## How to get there

- From Osaka: Sanyo Shinkansen Shin-Osaka–Hakata, Nozomi/Mizuho about 2h20–2h30 (Sakura/Hikari about 2h36, Kodama almost 5 h). 2026 fares: reserved ¥16,020 (Nozomi/Mizuho, normal season; ±¥200–400 by season), unreserved ¥14,750 for all trains.
- From Tokyo by shinkansen: direct Nozomi (Tokaido/Sanyo Shinkansen) about 5 h, around ¥23,000; Hikari/Sakura (Japan Rail Pass) add about 1 h and need a change at Shin-Osaka.
- From Tokyo by air: about 110 min flight, mostly from Haneda (a few from Narita), operated by JAL, ANA, Starflyer, Skymark, Peach and Jetstar Japan. Regular JAL/ANA/Starflyer fare around ¥45,000, discount fares ¥16,000–35,000; Skymark/Peach/Jetstar about ¥5,000–25,000. Lowest fares seen for Nov–Dec 2026: Peach ¥6,487, Jetstar ¥6,488, Starflyer ¥11,590, Skymark ¥11,760, JAL ¥15,084, ANA ¥15,540.
- Fukuoka Airport is a 5-minute subway ride from Hakata Station.
- Night bus from Tokyo (Shinjuku) by Nishitetsu: about 14.5 h, ¥9,000–25,000.

## Getting around

- Three subway lines link Fukuoka Airport, Hakata and Tenjin and most sights (e.g. Hakata–Kushida Shrine Station ¥210, Hakata–Ohori Koen ¥260). 1-Day Subway Pass ¥640 (calendar day, sold at subway ticket machines).
- IC cards Sugoca (JR Kyushu), Nimoca (Nishitetsu) and Hayakaken (subway) work on all trains, subways and buses in greater Fukuoka; Suica and other national IC cards are also accepted. They give no discount.
- Fukuoka Tourist City Pass (foreign tourists only, one calendar day, subway/JR/Nishitetsu trains and buses): ¥2,500 within the city, ¥2,800 including the Nishitetsu train to Dazaifu; rarely pays off against IC-card fares.
- Fukuoka Wide Pass ¥3,500: digital pass, 2 consecutive days on JR trains around Fukuoka Prefecture (to Shimonoseki and Karatsu); no shinkansen or subway.
- Most city buses are Nishitetsu; bus W1 runs between Tenjin, Fukuoka Dome, Fukuoka Tower and Fujisaki Station. Dazaifu: Nishitetsu train from Nishitetsu Fukuoka (Tenjin) Station via Futsukaichi, 25–40 min, ¥480; or Dazaifu Liner bus from Hakata Bus Center, about 40 min, ¥800.

## Local food

- Hakata ramen — thin noodles in creamy tonkotsu (pork bone) soup topped with chashu; at yatai stalls and many ramen shops around Hakata Station, and at the Ramen Stadium on Canal City's 5th floor.
- Mentaiko — spicy cod roe that came to Fukuoka via Korea; eaten raw or lightly seared with rice at yatai and izakaya.
- Motsunabe — beef or pork tripe hot pot with cabbage, garlic, chives and chili in soy- or miso-based soup, finished with noodles or rice.
- Tetsunabe gyoza — small pan-fried dumplings served in large batches on hot iron plates, at gyoza specialists especially around Hakata Station.
- Yatai food stalls — yakitori, oden and ramen at open-air stalls, best known along the river at the southern end of Nakasu Island (about 18:00–2:00).

## Seasons

- Late February to mid March: about 6,000 plum trees bloom at Dazaifu Tenmangu.
- Late March to early April: cherry blossoms in Maizuru Park around the Fukuoka Castle ruins, the city's main hanami spot.
- 1–15 July: Hakata Gion Yamakasa festival; decorative floats around the city 1–14 July and the float race at dawn on 15 July.
- Yatai stalls do not open in very bad weather (e.g. heavy rain or typhoons), and each stall closes one day a week.
