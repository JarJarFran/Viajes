# Kochi

Capital of Kochi Prefecture on the Pacific coast of Shikoku, known for Kochi Castle (one of twelve castles with an original keep and the only one that also keeps its original palace), katsuo no tataki (seared bonito) and the August Yosakoi dance festival. The city also has one of Japan's oldest street markets (the Sunday Market, since 1690) and Katsurahama beach with the statue of Sakamoto Ryoma.

## How to get there

- From Okayama: JR limited express Nanpu, about 2.5 hours (Tokyo-Okayama Shinkansen about 3.5 h; Tokyo-Kochi by rail about 6 h and about ¥21,000; covered by the Japan Rail Pass). JR Shikoku buses also run from the west side of Okayama Station to Kochi.
- From Tokyo by air: JAL and ANA from Haneda, Jetstar Japan from Narita, about 90 min; regular JAL/ANA fare around ¥41,000 one way, discount tickets ¥18,000-34,000, Jetstar from around ¥5,500. Kochi Ryoma Airport to JR Kochi Station by airport bus about 40 min (JNTO: about 30 min), ¥900.
- Overnight highway buses from Tokyo: about 12 hours, ¥8,900-14,500 depending on the day.
- Direct flights also from Osaka, Nagoya, Kobe and Fukuoka (JNTO).

## Getting around

- Tosaden trams: north-south Sanbashi Line and east-west Ino Line crossing at Harimayabashi; flat fare ¥230 per ride in the city centre, board at the rear, pay at the front; ask the driver for a transfer ticket (norikae-ken) when changing lines.
- My Yu Bus (tourist bus, roughly hourly): JR Kochi Station-Godaisan-Katsurahama; 1-day pass ¥900 (Kochi Station-Godaisan) or ¥1,300 (Kochi Station-Katsurahama).
- Kochiken Kotsu bus JR Kochi Station-Katsurahama: about 35 min, ¥800 one way.
- The castle, Harimayabashi, Hirome Market and the Sunday Market are all within about 10-20 min walk of JR Kochi Station.

## Local food

- Katsuo no tataki: bonito seared over a straw fire, sliced and served with spring onion, ginger and garlic with salt or ponzu-style dressing; at izakaya around Harimayabashi, at Hirome Market and at restaurants at Katsurahama.
- Hirome Market: indoor food hall with dozens of stalls and shared tables serving Kochi dishes and drinks, open from morning until late evening.
- Dorome: fresh young sardines, a local favourite with sake (there is an annual dorome festival with a sake-drinking contest).
- Imokenpi: crisp sugar-coated sweet potato sticks, a traditional snack sold as a souvenir and at the Sunday Market.
- Local sake: Kochi is known for its old sake breweries and a strong drinking culture.

## Seasons

- Climate: very wet humid subtropical climate with about twice the rainfall of Matsuyama and Takamatsu; the most typhoon-prone of Japan's major cities (typhoon season late summer to autumn). Winters are mild.
- Yosakoi Festival every year August 9-12 with about 20,000 dancers; hotels fill up and the Sunday Market is suspended on those Sundays.
- Kochi Castle is closed December 26 to January 1; the Kochi Castle Autumn Festival features kagura dance.
- Sunday Market does not open on January 1-2 even when they fall on a Sunday.
