# Matsuyama

Capital of Ehime Prefecture and the largest city on Shikoku. Known for Matsuyama Castle, one of Japan's twelve original castles on a hill in the city centre, and Dogo Onsen, one of Japan's oldest hot spring resorts with the 1894 Dogo Onsen Honkan bathhouse. The city is the setting of Natsume Soseki's novel 'Botchan' and the hometown of haiku poet Masaoka Shiki.

## How to get there

- From Okayama: JR limited express Shiokaze to Matsuyama, about 2.5 hours (Tokyo-Okayama Shinkansen about 3.5 h; Tokyo-Matsuyama by rail about 6.5 h and about ¥22,000 in total; covered by the Japan Rail Pass).
- From Tokyo by air: ANA and JAL from Haneda, about 90 min; regular fare around ¥47,000 one way, discount tickets typically ¥15,000-25,000; Jetstar Japan from Narita typically around ¥6,000.
- Overnight highway buses from Tokyo: regular fare around ¥12,000 one way (discount operators cheaper).
- Matsuyama Airport: airport bus to JR Matsuyama Station ¥980-1,000 (15 min), to Matsuyama-shi Station ¥980-1,000 (20 min), to Dogo Onsen ¥1,180-1,200 (35 min).
- By sea from Hiroshima: ferries arrive at Matsuyama Sightseeing Port, northwest of the centre; from the port take a short bus (2 min, ¥230-250) or walk 10-15 min to Takahama Station, then the Iyotetsu Takahama Line to Matsuyama-shi Station (20 min, ¥470-490).

## Getting around

- Iyotetsu trams (5 lines) link JR Matsuyama Station, Matsuyama-shi Station, Okaido (castle) and Dogo Onsen: flat fare ¥210 with an IC card or ¥230 in cash; board at the rear, pay when getting off. Tram line 5 JR Matsuyama Station-Dogo Onsen about 20 min; line 3 Matsuyama-shi Station-Dogo Onsen about 15 min.
- Iyotetsu buses: Dogo Onsen-Ishiteji 5 min (¥230 IC / ¥250 cash); IC cards save ¥20 per bus ride.
- Day-pass prices for the tram network were not confirmed in this research.

## Local food

- Matsuyama tart (taruto): sponge roll filled with red bean paste (sometimes yuzu or chestnut), a local confection since the 17th century; sold at souvenir shops and in the Dogo Onsen shopping arcade.
- Botchan dango: skewer of three mochi balls coated in matcha, egg and red-bean paste, named after the novel 'Botchan'; sold around Dogo Onsen.
- Mikan (Satsuma mandarins) and iyokan: Ehime is known for citrus; mandarin products (juice, sweets) are sold all over the city.
- Jakoten: fried patties of minced small fish, a speciality of Uwajima in southern Ehime, eaten grilled with soy sauce and grated daikon; common in Matsuyama izakaya and ryokan.
- Red sea bream (madai), Ehime's prefectural fish, is a staple of local seafood dishes.

## Seasons

- Climate: humid subtropical, hot summers and cool winters; rain heavier from April to July and in September.
- Cherry blossoms: about 200 trees on the castle grounds, typically late March to early April; Dogo Park (former castle site next to Dogo Onsen) is another popular spot.
- Matsuyama Castle keep and Ninomaru garden close on the third Wednesday of December and close earlier (16:30) in December-January; the ropeway runs every day.
- Festivals: Dogo Festival in spring, Matsuyama Festival in August and an autumn festival in October with clashing mikoshi (exact dates not confirmed here).
