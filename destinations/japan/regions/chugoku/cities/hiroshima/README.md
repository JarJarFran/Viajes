# Hiroshima

Principal city of the Chugoku Region (over one million inhabitants), known worldwide as the target of the first atomic bomb on 6 August 1945. Peace Memorial Park with its museum and the UNESCO-listed A-Bomb Dome forms the city centre, alongside the rebuilt Hiroshima Castle and Shukkeien Garden. The island of Miyajima with Itsukushima Shrine and its 'floating' torii is the usual side trip, under an hour away.

## How to get there

- From Tokyo: Tokaido/Sanyo Shinkansen, direct Nozomi ~4 h (Hikari/Sakura ~5 h with a change at Shin-Osaka); non-reserved ¥18,380, reserved ~¥19,000 (Hikari/Sakura) or ~¥19,500 (Nozomi). Japan Rail Pass valid on Hikari/Sakura; Nozomi needs a ¥6,500 supplement.
- From Shin-Osaka: Sanyo Shinkansen Nozomi/Mizuho ~1 h 20 min; non-reserved ¥9,890, reserved ¥10,950 (Nozomi/Mizuho, regular season), ¥10,420 (Sakura/Kodama). JR Kansai Hiroshima Area Pass: 5 days ¥17,000 (foreign tourists).
- By air: JAL/ANA from Haneda (~90 min flight), Spring Japan from Narita; Hiroshima Airport to the city is a ~50 min, ¥1,500 bus ride. Overnight highway buses from Tokyo take ~12 h (about ¥6,000–18,000).
- To Miyajima: JR Sanyo Line Hiroshima → Miyajimaguchi 25 min ¥420 (JR Pass valid) or tram line 2 (slower, ¥240), then JR or Matsudai ferry 10 min, ¥200 each way plus the ¥100 Miyajima visitor tax (included in regular tickets/IC fares; rail-pass holders buy a ¥100 tax ticket at the terminal). Direct boats from Peace Park: 45 min, ¥2,400 one way / ¥4,400 round trip (excluding tax).

## Getting around

- Trams (Hiroden, Japan's largest tram network, 9 lines): flat fare ¥240 per ride, paid on exit; IC cards (Suica, Icoca, ...) accepted; JR Pass not valid.
- Tram 1-day pass ¥700 (calendar day); for ¥300 more (¥1,000) a 1-day pass that adds the Matsudai ferry to Miyajima and back including the ¥100 visitor tax. Pass holders get the Miyajima Ropeway round trip for ¥1,500 instead of ¥2,000. Available as digital tickets.
- Hiroshima Sightseeing Loop Bus 'Meipuru-pu' (JR): every 10–15 min past the main sights; ¥240 per ride or ¥600 1-day pass; covered by the Japan Rail Pass.
- On foot: Hiroshima Station → Shukkeien → Hiroshima Castle → Peace Memorial Park is about 3 km.

## Local food

- Hiroshima-style okonomiyaki — thin batter, lots of cabbage layered over yakisoba noodles, cooked on a griddle in front of counter diners; many shops in Okonomimura (downtown) and around Hiroshima Station.
- Oysters — Hiroshima Prefecture produces roughly 60–70% of Japan's oysters; served raw, deep-fried, grilled or on rice in Hiroshima and on Miyajima (also from food stands).
- Momiji manju — maple-leaf-shaped cakes filled with sweet red bean paste (also matcha, custard, cheese, chocolate), created on Miyajima in the early 1900s; sold on Miyajima and in Hiroshima.
- Hiroshima tsukemen — cold noodles dipped in a spicy red-pepper sauce, with a selectable spiciness level.

## Seasons

- Autumn colours on Miyajima (Momijidani): estimated best viewing mid–late November (2026 season).
- 6 August: Peace Memorial Ceremony in Peace Memorial Park (moment of silence at 8:15); the Peace Memorial Museum stays open until 20:00 in August.
- Year-end closures: Peace Memorial Museum 30–31 Dec (plus three days in mid-February), Shukkeien 29–31 Dec, Hiroshima Castle Ninomaru 29 Dec–2 Jan. Itsukushima Shrine and Daisho-in have no closing days.
- Mazda Museum (factory tours, weekdays, free, online reservation) is closed for renovation 8–26 Feb 2027; the Itsukushima Shrine five-storied pagoda is under renovation until Nov 2027 (scaffolding to be removed Dec 2026).
